How to weigh in

Residents

  • Contact your state senator and your state representative. Pick your city, community or county above to see who they are.
  • Give the bill number, your position, and a reason.
  • In committee: write to the committee or testify at its hearing, and ask the chair to bring the bill up (or not). On the calendar: ask your legislators to vote for it or against it. Presented to the governor: write the governor, who has 10 days.
  • The Legislature meets from January to late March or early April each year, with a veto session in late April; bills stay alive through both years of a biennium.

Organizations and businesses

  • Testify at committee hearings for your organization, as a proponent, opponent or neutral party; written testimony goes to the committee's assistant before the hearing.
  • Many bills are introduced by a committee at someone's request: ask a committee's chair or a member to request one.
  • For the budget, the House budget committees and the Senate Ways and Means subcommittees hear agencies in January and February.
  • Lobbyists register with the Secretary of State.

Counties, cities and school districts

  • Send a resolution or letter to your legislators, the sponsors, and the committee; after it passes both houses, write the governor's office.
  • Ask your legislators about bills that affect local taxes and budgets early, before turnaround in late February.
