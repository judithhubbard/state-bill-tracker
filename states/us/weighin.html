Residents, organizations and local governments

Residents

  • Contact your representative and your two senators. Pick your state and place above to see who they are.
  • Give the bill number, your position, and a reason. The contact forms ask for your address, so the office knows you're a constituent.
  • In committee: ask your members to cosponsor it, and write to the committee's chair. On the calendar: ask your members to vote for it or against it. On the President's desk: write the White House, which has 10 days (Sundays excepted).
  • Congress meets most weeks of the year except in August and before elections; bills stay alive for the whole two-year Congress.

Organizations and businesses

  • Ask a member to introduce a bill, or to cosponsor one; a bill with sponsors from both parties and both houses goes further.
  • Submit written testimony when a committee holds a hearing; the committee's website lists hearings and how to submit.
  • For spending, the Appropriations committees take members' requests in the spring for the fiscal year that starts October 1.
  • Lobbyists register with the Clerk of the House and the Secretary of the Senate.

Counties, cities and tribes

  • Send a resolution or letter to your delegation, the sponsors and the committee; after a bill passes both houses, write the White House.
  • Ask your members early about bills that affect local funding, before the committee's markup.

The White House

Online: whitehouse.gov/contact

Mail: The White House, 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue NW, Washington, DC 20500.
