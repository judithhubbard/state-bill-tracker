The steps

  1. Introduction. A member, or a joint interim committee, files a bill (many are filed before the session). The Legislature meets each year: a general session in odd years (January to early March, 40 legislative days at most) and a budget session in even years (February to early March, 20 at most). In a budget session, a bill other than the budget needs two-thirds of its house to be introduced. Bills that don't pass by the end of the session die.
  2. Committee. The Speaker or the Senate President sends the bill to a standing committee on its subject. The committee takes public comment and votes on whether to recommend it ("do pass"). Bills that spend money often go on to the Appropriations Committee.
  3. Floor. A recommended bill goes on the general file. The full house debates and amends it as the Committee of the Whole, then votes on second and third reading. It needs a majority of all members: 32 in the House, 16 in the Senate.
  4. The other house. The bill goes to the other house and starts again in committee. If the second house amends it, the first has to agree ("concur"), or a joint conference committee works out the differences.
  5. The governor. During the session, the governor has 3 days (Sundays excepted) to sign or veto a bill; bills that arrive at the end get 15 days after adjournment. The governor can veto items in an appropriation bill. A bill the governor doesn't act on becomes law. Overriding a veto takes two-thirds of each house.
  6. Constitutional amendments. The Legislature proposes an amendment to the Wyoming Constitution with a joint resolution (HJ or SJ). It goes through committees and both floors like a bill, but needs two-thirds of each house's members, and it skips the governor: it goes to the voters at the next general election in November of an even year, where it needs a majority of everyone voting in the election.
  7. The calendar. Many new laws take effect July 1; others immediately or on a date the bill sets.

How to have an impact

  • Start before the session. Many bills come from the joint interim committees, which meet across the state between sessions and take public comment. That's often the best time to shape a bill.
  • Comment at the committee meeting. Committees take public comment on bills, in person and often online; the Legislature's website lists meetings and how to take part.
  • Move quickly. Sessions are short, and a bill a committee doesn't report by the cutoff dies.
  • Bring in your own legislators. You have one state representative and one state senator, and they listen most to their own constituents. Ask them to co-sponsor a bill and to vote for it.
  • Write again only with something new, such as a new fact, a local impact, or a new stage ("it just passed the House; please sign it"). The same message twice from the same person usually counts once.
  • Multiply people, not messages. Ten different people writing outweighs one person writing ten times.
  • Make it personal. A sentence about why it matters to you or your community carries more weight than form text.
  • If it runs out of time, ask the sponsors or an interim committee to bring it back in a later session.

About this data

Where the data comes from: the Legislative Service Office's data service, the same one wyoleg.gov uses (each bill's history, sponsors, summaries and roll calls; the members and committees), updated daily, and the Census Bureau's district relationship files for cities, towns and counties.

House Bills and Senate Files of the regular sessions are shown, with the joint resolutions that propose constitutional amendments; other resolutions and special sessions are left out. Topics come from the committees a bill was sent to and the words in its title, so a bill can have more than one.

“What it does” is the Legislative Service Office's bill summary where there is one (mostly for laws), else the clauses of the bill's official title. Votes list how each member voted, in committee and on the floor.

Governor's deadlines are estimates from the Constitution's rule (3 days during the session, Sundays excepted, and 15 days after it).
