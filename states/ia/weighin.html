Residents, organizations and local governments

Residents

  • Write your state representative and your state senator ().
  • Give the bill number, your position, and a reason. Every legislator has an email address; the Capitol phones are each house's switchboard, which takes messages.
  • In committee: go to, or write the members of, the subcommittee's public meeting (each bill gets one), and ask the committee chair to take it up before the funnel deadline. Passed both houses: write the governor, who has 30 days after the session.
  • Bills carry over from the first year to the second, but the funnel deadlines come in February and March each year, so act early.

Organizations and businesses

  • Ask a member to file your idea, or a committee chair to take it up as a study bill, before the session opens in January.
  • Follow the bill through its renumberings: a committee that approves it usually gives it a new number.
  • Lobbyists register with the House and Senate and file a declaration for each bill they lobby on; the declarations are public.

Cities and counties

  • Work through the Iowa League of Cities and the Iowa State Association of Counties, which set legislative priorities each year.
