# Site audit, 2026-11-17

Five reviews (front page and issue pages, state pages, data accuracy, technical, visuals), merged and ranked. Screenshots
and raw notes were in the session's scratch folder (audit-front, audit-state, audit-data, audit-tech, audit-visual).

## 1. Wrong now (errors)

Data (fixes started 2026-11-17, except where noted):
- Connecticut 2023 empty on the live site (all bills stage 0, no actions); local data is right (235 laws, 5 vetoes).
- Dates from the wrong part of a line or source typos: VT H.790 (delivered "2027"), NH laws dated by their effective date
  (2027-01-01), WV SB239 (year 2202), 17 GA laws dated by "Effective Date" lines, MA four signings dated 2026-11-30.
- PA titles with HTML entities ("&#8211;"), "COSPONSOR MEMO -" prefixes, all-caps titles; HI "?" for ʻokina/apostrophe.
- Blank-title placeholder bills (21 NJ 2026, NH HB8002-8012).
- Montana's front-page party numbers are the members-elect (contacts_2027), not the members in office.
- Florida's front-page numbers (25-13, 70-50) are the 2026-28 member list read 11-14: check against the election.
- Issue pages count Illinois's previous General Assembly's bills in the new year (the end-of-assembly line keeps them
  "alive"): IL 2023 abortion 73 bills, 35 of them from 2021-22. Not started (issues_site.py busy).
- Issue maps say Indiana, Utah, Virginia had "no regular session in 2023": they meet yearly; the site lacks the data.

State pages:
- Finished sessions still read as running (OK: "dies when the session ends in May 2026"; VT contradicts itself; OK and MS
  label Out of time rows "In committee" where TX says "Died in committee").
- Constitutional amendments "Still in play / Going to the voters" a year after the vote (TX's 17 from Nov 2025, OK, NE).
- A bill whose identical twin became law is told "ask the sponsor to reintroduce it" (VT H407 / S45).
- "This one is more likely to move: it got further" on laws, vetoes and dead bills.
- Massachusetts: a bill "sent to study" shown as waiting for a committee vote, with odds.
- Congress: no one to contact for a House bill awaiting a floor vote (should be the Speaker); Senate picker "Choose your
  district"; "Agawam Town city", "Dakota City city" place names.
- Vermont: "your county" in a town-based state.

Front page:
- "Open Texas from January's bills" (the 2027 party view's panel link).
- Search: Maine's bills shown as "HLD 307" (should be LD 307); vetoed bills sorted before laws.
- Issue pages' legend always shows "Not on the site yet".
- "Supermajority" means enough to override a veto, which the page never says: WV (32-2) and AL look like bugs.
- Panel text "Student personal information.: The bill would ..." (doubled punctuation).

Technical:
- State pages always carry a noindex tag, even after launch (build_data.py's github_page()): a launch blocker.
- No 404 page: every bad address returns the front page with status 200.
- Front page throws when browser storage is blocked (drawMine's loop); search box's aria-expanded needs role=combobox;
  map tiles' spoken names don't start with their visible code; dark-mode white text on the middle blue fails contrast.

## 2. Improvements

Ease of use (the owner's worry: pages too busy for someone new to government):
- State pages: one simplification pass. Drop the "Democrats hold both houses ..." sentence; the status line repeated on
  every row ("Not meeting: dies December 31 ...") said once; fewer tags per bill row (party dots, topics, earlier sessions
  into the opened bill); "New here?" once, then a link; advanced filters under "More filters"; a simple funnel leading
  Statistics, denser charts under "Explore".
- Join the state pages' 14 topics with the front page's issues (same groups; issue tags on bills linking to issue pages).
- Raw legislature wording in status lines (Congress's "Reported by Senator Cruz with an amendment in the nature of a
  substitute ..."; OK "Coauthored by ..."): plain words in the row, the full text under Every action.
- Contact details shown twice on finished-session panels.
- Phone: the state page's tab bar wraps to three rows.

Visuals (one site, not three):
- Blue means Democratic on party view but "more bills" on issue maps: give the amount ramp another hue (teal or grey).
- "Became law" green on the front page, gold on issue pages, gold for other things on state pages: green = law, gold =
  yours, everywhere.
- Three party palettes (front map, state header chips, bill tags); "both parties" dot reads as red.
- Democratic vs Split hard to tell apart; one-party tiles very pale.
- Issue tiles: the state code jumps up when a law badge is present (rows don't line up).
- Front page, issue pages and state pages use different controls, widths and fonts (monospace on state pages): one shared
  stylesheet.
- Statistics charts small with colliding labels; the network chart is unlabeled grey dots.

Speed:
- New York and Congress take ~18 s to become usable on a typical phone (2.8-3 MB of data); Texas 10 s. Load summaries and
  full histories on demand, as bill texts already are.
- A search naming no state downloads 3.8 MB; show results as states arrive, or a small combined index.
- Nothing cached in the browser; fonts from Google (self-host).

## 3. Missed opportunities
- No line under the title saying what the site is for.
- No link to the issues index from the front page.
- Phones can't see party control details (the panel needs hover).
- Ballot measures: link the official results instead of "the records here don't show the result".
- Texas between sessions: link the 2027 prefiled bills and "find your legislators" in the notice.
- A dead bill that's back next session: lead with "It's back as HB 1011".
- Social previews, sitemap, analytics on the front and issue pages (launch checklist).

## 4. Checked and fine
- Laws and vetoes match recorded official totals in about 30 states and Congress; ~25 bills spot-checked against sources.
- All states rebuilt today; no stale data. No other state's wording leaking in. No console errors; no broken links in a
  sample of 100; no sideways scroll at 375px. The front page loads in 0.6 s on a phone.
