State Bill Tracker

About

Every year, state legislatures and Congress consider well over 100,000 bills. Most people never hear about a bill until it has passed or failed, and by then it’s too late to weigh in.

State Bill Tracker makes the process visible: you can follow the bills being considered, see what step comes next, and reach the person who makes that next decision. It covers all 50 state legislatures and Congress.

Why I built it

I’m Judith Hubbard. I’m an earthquake scientist, and I serve as a county legislator. From both, I know that good decisions depend on good information, and that the legislative process is much easier to influence when you can see it. This site is meant to make legislation more transparent, and to give anyone a way to follow the bills they care about and speak up while it still matters. The power of government lies with the governed – or at least, it should.

Data sources

  • Bills, votes and summaries: legislatures’ own public records wherever possible, supplemented with data from Open States and from LegiScan (used under a Creative Commons Attribution license).
  • Congress: the Government Publishing Office, the House Clerk and the Senate.
  • Past party control: the National Conference of State Legislatures.
  • District maps: the U.S. Census Bureau.

Each state’s page lists its own sources.

Legislatures’ records have errors and gaps, and so may this site. If something looks wrong, please let me know.

Your privacy

Your watchlist and settings stay in your own browser. The site doesn’t ask for your name or email, and doesn’t sell or share anything about you. It counts visits with GoatCounter, which doesn’t track individuals.
