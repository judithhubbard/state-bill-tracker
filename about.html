About State Bill Tracker

Every two years, state legislatures and Congress consider well over 100,000 bills. Most people never hear about a bill until it has passed or failed, and by then it’s too late to weigh in.

How can you use this tracker to make a difference?

There are a lot of steps between a bill being proposed and becoming law. A bill may need to pass several committees, and each committee chair has to bring it up for a vote. A bill that has passed committee may still need a vote in each house. And then the governor has to sign it. Across the 50 states, about three in five bills never make it out of committee, and only about one in five becomes law.

Here, we make each step visible, and tell you who to contact, and when. Ask your legislators to support a bill (or oppose it). Ask a committee chair to call the bill up. Ask the governor to sign. At each of these steps, your voice has an impact.

Each state’s Statistics tab shows where its bills usually stall. So star the bills you care about to build your watchlist, check back as they move, and share your list with friends, neighbors and groups who care about the same issues.

Your privacy

Your watchlist and settings stay in your own browser. If you share a list, its bills, its title and any notes you add are stored so others can see them; nothing else about you is. The site doesn’t ask for your name or email, and doesn’t sell or share anything about you. We do count visits with GoatCounter, which doesn’t track individuals.

Data sources

  • Bills, votes and summaries: legislatures’ own public records wherever possible, supplemented with data from Open States and from LegiScan (used under a Creative Commons Attribution license).
  • Congress: the Government Publishing Office, the House Clerk and the Senate.
  • Party control: the legislatures’ member lists; past years from the National Conference of State Legislatures; next year’s from reported election results.
  • District maps: the U.S. Census Bureau.

Each state’s page lists its own sources.

Legislatures’ records have errors and gaps, and so may this site. If something looks wrong, please let me know.
